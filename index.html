Business finance · 6 min read

Five financial signals every founder should understand before their next growth move.

Scaling a business requires a clear understanding of its underlying economic health. Monitor these five critical financial metrics before committing capital to a new growth phase.

1. LTV:CAC Ratio (Lifetime Value vs. Customer Acquisition Cost)

  • This metric measures the relationship between how much it costs to acquire a new client and the total revenue that client generates during their relationship with your firm.
  • A healthy benchmark is typically 3:1 or higher. If your ratio is closer to 1:1, accelerating growth will rapidly deplete your capital because you are spending almost as much to get a customer as they are worth.

2. Cash Runway and Monthly Burn Rate

  • Your burn rate is the amount of cash your company loses each month, and your runway is how many months you can survive at that rate before running out of money entirely.
  • Before launching a major expansion or hiring push, verify that you have an adequate runway (typically 12 to 18 months) to absorb the upfront costs of growth before those investments start generating returns.

3. Gross Margin Stability

  • Gross margin reveals what percentage of revenue remains after deducting the direct costs of delivering your services.
  • If your gross margins are shrinking as your revenue grows, scaling will only multiply your operational inefficiencies. Ensure your unit economics are profitable and stable before accelerating sales.

4. Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)

  • This tracks how many days it takes for your company to convert its investments, like payroll or software licenses, into cash flow from sales.
  • If clients take 60 to 90 days to pay invoices but your payroll hits every 14 days, rapid top-line growth will severely drain your cash reserves. A shorter cycle ensures you have the liquidity to fund ongoing operations.

5. Net Revenue Retention (NRR)

  • NRR measures the percentage of recurring revenue retained from existing customers over a specific period, factoring in expansions, upsells, downgrades, and churn.
  • An NRR above 100% indicates that your current client base is increasing its spend faster than you are losing revenue to churn. High retention proves your service delivers lasting value, creating a safe foundation for top-of-funnel growth.

Technology · 4 min read

What a good digital roadmap looks like when you’re growing fast.

A digital roadmap for a rapidly scaling business ditches rigid timelines in favor of dynamic prioritization. It must link every technology investment directly to revenue growth, operational leverage, or risk mitigation.

The “Now, Next, Later” Framework

Instead of plotting specific dates 18 months out—which will inevitably change—fast-growing companies structure their roadmaps in three rolling horizons:

  • Now (0–3 months): Critical items currently in development. Focus on immediate bottleneck resolution, such as automating a manual onboarding process or migrating to a more resilient database.
  • Next (3–9 months): High-confidence initiatives that require scoping. Examples include launching a client-facing mobile app or integrating predictive analytics into your CRM.
  • Later (9+ months): Strategic ambitions. These are North Star goals, like entering a new market with a localized tech stack or completely overhauling legacy enterprise resource planning (ERP) software.

Compliance · 5 min read

GST and beyond: building a compliance rhythm that never holds you back.

For fast-growing Indian enterprises, treating statutory compliance as an end-of-year accounting panic guarantees operational friction, frozen working capital, and heavy penalties. A predictable compliance rhythm transforms regulatory filings from a bottleneck into a seamless background process.

The monthly cash & tax cadence

  • 7th of the month (TDS): Deposit Tax Deducted at Source on time to avoid a 1.5% monthly interest penalty on late deposits.
  • 11th & 20th (GST returns): File GSTR-1 (outward supplies) by the 11th and GSTR-3B (summary and tax payment) by the 20th. Delayed GST filings not only attract late fees but can block your ability to generate e-way bills.
  • 15th (PF & ESI): Deposit employee provident fund and insurance contributions to maintain strict labour law compliance and protect employee trust.

The 45-day MSME payment rule (Section 43B(h))

  • Outstanding payments to registered Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs) must be cleared within 15 days (if there is no written agreement) or 45 days (with a written agreement).
  • If you miss this window, those unpaid vendor bills cannot be claimed as tax deductions for that year, which artificially inflates your taxable income and severely drains your cash reserves. Run a vendor audit by the 28th of every month to flag aging MSE invoices.

Quarterly & annual checkpoints

  • Advance tax: Avoid heavy year-end tax shocks by paying your corporate tax in cumulative quarterly installments (15th of June, September, December, and March).
  • ROC filings: Missing Form AOC-4 (financial statements) and MGT-7 (annual returns) triggers a ₹100-per-day compounding penalty. A typical company missing these and a few GST returns can easily rack up ₹50,000+ in pure late fees.
